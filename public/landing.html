How It Works

You write on paper. The phone only reads. Four steps, and the longest one is the writing.

1

Tap the cover

The chip is in the leather. Touch your phone to it and today’s prompt is already open — no app to hunt for, no password. The book is the key.

2

Write it by hand

Then put the phone down. Ink on paper, or speak it aloud, or draw it — whichever the day calls for. Nothing is timed and nothing is counting.

3

Photograph the page

One photo. Claude reads your handwriting off the paper — or your voice, or the colour and composition — and tells you what it sees, including what you circled and left.

4

The pattern shows up

One entry is a mood. Six is a pattern. Καιρός tracks what keeps returning across weeks — and says so plainly when nothing has shifted.

Built in Austria

From Coffee Shop to AI Innovation

Why someone with no software background spent two years building a journal, and how it got made.

Made in Austria

"Everyone should be able to write their own book"

I was studying chemistry in Georgia when a car accident ended that. I moved to Austria at 26, spent two years learning German well enough to study in it, and started again — teacher training, Chemistry and English. At 30 I took leave from it to build this.

The idea came from two places at once. I was reading Marcus Aurelius' Meditations — a private notebook, never written to be read, which is exactly why it still lands. And I was watching my mother journal every day, the same way, for years. What if the patterns she was writing down could be read back to her? What if anyone could keep their own Meditations and have something notice what runs through it?

I funded it working part-time at a coffee shop in a small Austrian village. Every version of this app was built between shifts.

"I had never shipped software. What I had was a clear idea and Claude as a development partner — which is not the same as taking whatever it writes. The first pass at the journey prompts came back generic, so I rewrote them, and I still read every prompt before it ships. Deciding what belongs in someone's journal is the work. The syntax was the part I could delegate."

No testimonials yet. Here's the work instead.

Καιρός is early. Rather than borrow credibility it hasn't earned, here is precisely what has been built — all of it verifiable.

1,047

Prompts, written one at a time

Not templates with the nouns swapped. All 53 paths are hand-authored day by day — 50 included with every journal, plus three in the Moments pack — running from four-day sprints to hundred-day odysseys, each prompt written for its specific day and theme.

3

Languages, all the way down

English, German, and Georgian — and not just the buttons. All 1,047 journey prompts are translated, and the AI writes its analysis back to you in your language. Georgian gets its own typeface so the script renders properly rather than falling back.

3

Ways to make an entry

Photograph your handwriting, speak it aloud, or paint and sketch it. Claude reads all three — transcribing the page, transcribing the audio, or interpreting colour and composition when there are no words at all. Multi-modal paths ask for more than one in a single journey.

Your API key never touches the browser — every Claude call is proxied server-side. Entries are never used to train models.
